Your play library

Keep it child-led

A happy starting point

  • Ask or observe whether your child wants to join. Offer a choice of two toys and an easy way to say “stop”.
  • Start with one short round and rest. The suggested times are play starting points, not exercise doses.
  • Change one thing at a time: distance, support, speed or texture. End while play is still comfortable.
  • Use stable surfaces and large, age-safe toys. Keep small parts, balloons, batteries and magnets away from children who may mouth objects.
Individual support

Comfort comes first

Stop for pain, dizziness, nausea, unusual breathlessness, pallor, loss of balance, or distress. Do not force movement, touch, eye contact or sound exposure.

For cerebral palsy, use the child’s prescribed seating, orthoses or mobility aid. Ask their clinician about weight-bearing, joint range, fatigue, hip concerns and safe transfers. Do not pull on arms or improvise lifting or restraint.

For ASD or ADHD, adapt to the individual: predictable steps, communication supports, quiet space, breaks and preferred interests can help. A diagnosis alone does not identify sensory preferences or mobility ability.
